## What a brand book contains (common structure)
Cover with wordmark → numbered chapters: 01 brand story and voice, 02 logo (clear space, minimum size, misuse), 03 colour (primary, secondary, neutrals, with values), 04 typography (pairing, scale, specimens), 05 imagery and iconography, 06 components and applications (screens, social, merch). Strong books use one bold accent against a lot of neutral space, big chapter numbers, and real application mock-ups rather than abstract swatches. `DESIGN_BRIEF.md` will follow this order.

## Patterns observed
- **Cinematic dark movie apps:** near-black backgrounds, poster art doing all the colour work, glassy cards, carousels of posters. Very common, so it's the easiest direction to make generic. If used, Truce needs one distinctive idea, not another streaming-app clone.
- **Film-festival identities:** typography-led, condensed or very heavy type, one or two loud colours, grids and frames, laurel/credit-roll conventions. Strong personality without relying on poster images.
- **Ticket stubs:** perforated edges, admit-one framing, serial numbers, tear-off parts. A natural metaphor for Truce's single pick ("your ticket for tonight") and the veto ("tear it, next one").
- **Warm minimal UI:** cream and paper backgrounds, serif headlines, soft depth, generous spacing. Calm and friendly; reads well in daylight.
- **Group / social apps:** avatar stacks, presence dots, progress of "who's ready". Useful for the lobby, but the playful-3D-avatar look is overused.

## Takeaways for Truce
1. The reveal is the hero moment: one film, big, with per-person reasons. Every direction must design that screen first.
2. A metaphor beats decoration: ticket, verdict, handshake/peace, credits.
3. Posters come from TMDb and vary wildly in colour, so the UI palette must stay calm enough not to clash.
4. Avoid: purple-blue AI gradients, generic glassmorphism movie-app layouts, 3D cartoon avatars, emoji-led UI.
